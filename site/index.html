Your Switch games.
Native on your Mac.

AetherNX rewrites a game's own code once, on your Mac, so Apple silicon runs it directly. No emulator, no JIT: what comes out is an app of that game, for the Mac, the iPhone and the iPad.

Not emulated. Rewritten.

The Switch and Apple silicon speak the same processor language, 64-bit ARM. So a game's code does not need to be translated, instruction by instruction, while you play. It needs to be made welcome.

Rewritten once
A few instructions break Apple's rules: a Switch game uses the register x18 freely, and on Apple platforms it belongs to the system. When you build a game, the rewriter moves each of them into a small stub beside the code. It happens once, on your Mac.
A host for the console's system
When the game asks for a file, a thread or a frame on screen, HorizonNative answers in place of Horizon OS, with the Mac's own services.
Drawn with Metal
The Switch's graphics API, NVN, is implemented on Metal, and every shader the game carries is translated to Metal ahead of time, so a scene does not stutter the first time it appears.

One window for your whole library.

AetherNX Studio keeps your games, their builds, saves, mods and cheats in one Mac window that grows with your display: a home with the game you played last, every game as a tile, and a page for each.

Everything about a game, on its page.

The files it is built from, what was built from them, the save on this Mac and the one in iCloud, its mods and its cheats. On a wide display the page opens into two columns; in a narrow window the buttons fold into icons.

A build that tells you what it is doing.

Step 5 of 9, what that step does and why, the time left, and the steps already done. When it ends well it says so and gets out of the way; when it does not, it says what went wrong and offers to try again.

Two parts that update on their own.

The app updates itself from its signed, notarized releases. The engine that builds your games updates in seconds, without a new app, and the Updates page says what each change means: a rebuild for engine fixes, shaders translated again, or nothing to do.

Settings in the same window, and a setup that explains itself.

Settings are a page, not another window, with saves, mods and cheats explained where you set them. Before your first build, the Setup Assistant checks what your Mac needs (Xcode, Homebrew, Python and the build's own tools), says what each piece is for, and installs what is missing.

And on your iPhone and iPad.

Install on a device straight from the Mac: AetherNX builds the game, signs it with your own Apple team, free or paid, and installs it over the cable or Wi-Fi. No sideloading tool, no Xcode window.

A large game's files can stay on the device. Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave copies its 27 GB of data once; after that, installing again, for an engine update say, copies only the app.

Saves, mods and cheats, in iCloud.

Each game gets a folder with its name in iCloud Drive. Its save syncs through it to every Mac, iPhone and iPad of yours. Its mods and cheats sit beside it, so every Mac of yours plays with the same ones; the iPhone and iPad apps get them next.

Four steps to your first game.

  1. Download and open AetherNX

    The Setup Assistant checks your Mac and installs what the builds need, saying why for each piece.

  2. Choose your keys

    The prod.keys dumped from your own Switch. They stay on your Mac, never in the apps.

  3. Add your games

    One folder per game, with the base game, its newest update and its DLC.

  4. Build, then Play

    The first build takes 30 to 60 minutes; later ones reuse most of it. Or press Install for your iPhone or iPad.

Prefer the terminal? Clone it and go:

git clone https://github.com/iCosiSenpai/AetherNX.git ~/AetherNX
cd ~/AetherNX && ./aethernx setup && ./aethernx doctor
./aethernx build ~/Games/your game          # a Mac app
./aethernx install ~/Games/your game        # on your iPhone or iPad

Liner notes.

AetherNX stands on years of other people's work, given freely. To every one of them: thank you.

The engine

nxnative by seedds and its contributors, which did the hard part and keeps doing it: HorizonNative is its fork. It began as d2r-mobile.

Inside it: the Ryujinx team's shader translator, Khronos' SPIRV-Cross and Vulkan headers, libvpx, dav1d, stb_vorbis, deko3d's texture layout and NVIDIA's FLIP.

Front ends that showed the way

NX2Apple by LNPANDY, whose device install and cloud saves AetherNX follows. NX Library by Bium23, for its mods, cheats and build progress. LumaNX by Xc1Ym and NX OS by tripathishubham1.

Research and tools

nxrt by fedebuonco, switchbrew, Atmosphère's cheat format, the decompilation work of open-ead and zeldaret, libimobiledevice, Homebrew, and the artwork of nlib.cc and Wikipedia.
